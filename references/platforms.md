# Platforms: same voice, different room

The photographer's voice is constant (see [brand-voice.md](brand-voice.md)); the
format is not. The same caption pasted to every platform is a wasted post four
times over, because each one rewards a different shape. Draft per platform, not
once-and-copy.

## Instagram

The home base for most photographers. Four surfaces, and they are not
interchangeable:

- **Feed (single image)** — the hero. Caption can breathe: a strong first line
  (see [hooks-and-hashtags.md](hooks-and-hashtags.md)), then a short story or a
  point, then a soft CTA. The first line is what shows before "more", so front-load
  it.
- **Carousel** — the workhorse for educational and social-proof. Slide one is the
  hook and has to earn the swipe; each slide is one idea; the last slide is the
  CTA. Carousels get re-surfaced and saved, so they punch above their weight.
- **Reels** — reach. A hook in the first two seconds or they scroll. Behind-the-
  scenes, transformations, quick tips. Write the on-screen hook text and a short
  caption; the video does the work, the caption supports it.
- **Stories** — the daily, disposable, human layer. Polls, "getting set up",
  reposts, a booking-link sticker. Not part of the permanent grid; treat as glue,
  not headline content.

Hashtags: in the caption or the first comment, both fine. Keep them relevant (see
hashtags reference). Caption length is flexible but the first line is everything.

## TikTok

Reach engine, and unforgiving about the hook — the first second decides. Native,
unpolished, fast beats produced-and-precious. Behind-the-scenes, quick education,
personality. Captions are short; the hook lives on-screen and in the audio. A
polished ad reads as an ad and dies; a real moment travels. Trends and sounds
matter here in a way they don't elsewhere — but never force the photographer into
a trend that isn't them.

## LinkedIn

Underrated, and the best room in the building for **corporate and headshot**
photographers, because the buyer is right there — HR leads, office managers,
founders. The register is more composed: no lowercase-casual, lighter on emoji,
led by insight or value. "What a dated headshot signals to a candidate", "why we
shot a whole leadership team in one morning". Personal and educational posts do
well; hero shots alone do not. This is where a headshot photographer's next
corporate booking actually reads their content.

## Facebook

Skews to established, local, referral-driven audiences and community groups. Warm,
plain, a little longer, links are fine (unlike Instagram). Strong for local
family/portrait work and for reaching the people who already know you. Cross-posting
from Instagram works here more than anywhere, but a native, slightly warmer tone
lands better.

## Pinterest

Not a social network — a search engine, and a slow, compounding one. Pins keep
driving traffic for months, which is the opposite of the Instagram half-life. Keyword-
rich descriptions, vertical images, tall educational graphics ("what to wear for
your headshot"). Best for portrait, wedding, and family photographers whose clients
plan and search. Write for search intent, not for the feed.

## Choosing where to be

More platforms is not better. A photographer posting well on two beats one posting
thinly on five. Pick the rooms where the actual buyer is — LinkedIn for corporate
headshots, Pinterest and Instagram for weddings and families, Facebook for local
referral markets — and set the per-platform cadence in `build_calendar.py`
accordingly. An empty account is worse than no account; only commit to the cadence
the photographer will actually keep.
