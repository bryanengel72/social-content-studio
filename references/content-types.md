# Content Types: the mix, and why a month needs one

The single most common mistake in a photographer's feed is that every post is the
same post: here is a pretty photo, here is another pretty photo. It photographs
beautifully and it converts nothing, because a feed of hero shots gives a stranger
no reason to trust you, no reason to learn from you, and no idea how to book you.

A month of content works when the posts do **different jobs**. `build_calendar.py`
rotates through the types below so the shape of the month is varied before a word
is written; this file is what each type is *for*, so the caption actually does the
job the slot was created for.

## The six types and their jobs

| Type | The job | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **hero** | Reach + desire | The standout frame. Stops the scroll, shows the ceiling of your work. The one people share. |
| **educational** | Authority + saves | Teach something the client needs: what to wear for headshots, how to prep a team, why lighting matters. Saved and sent, which the algorithm rewards. |
| **behind-the-scenes** | Trust + relatability | The setup, the bloopers, the reality behind the polished frame. Makes you a person, not a logo. |
| **social-proof** | Trust → booking | A result, a testimonial (with consent), a before/after of confidence not just image. The nudge for someone on the fence. |
| **personal** | Connection | You: why you shoot, a story, a value, a real moment. People book people. This is the one AI writes worst — it needs a true thing only the photographer knows. |
| **cta** | Conversion | The direct ask. Booking open, how to book, a date to hold. The post that turns an audience into revenue. |

## Why the ratio matters more than any single post

Roughly: **reach** posts (hero, educational) bring new eyes, **trust** posts
(behind-the-scenes, social-proof, personal) turn those eyes into belief, and the
**cta** turns belief into a booking. Skip a layer and the funnel leaks:

- All hero → a pretty account nobody books from.
- All educational → useful, but no personality and no ask.
- All cta → exhausting, and it reads as desperate. Nobody follows a billboard.

The default rotation lands a CTA roughly every sixth post — frequent enough to
actually sell, rare enough that the other posts have earned the right to ask. If
the photographer is launching something (mini-sessions, a booking window), it's
fine to raise the CTA rate for that stretch; the rest of the time, keep it earned.

## Matching type to source

A single shoot can feed several types: the best frame is a hero, the setup photo
is behind-the-scenes, the client's reaction is social-proof, and "here's what we
did to make a nervous exec relax" is educational. One good session is a week of
varied content if it's mined for more than its prettiest picture. Note the source
shoot in the calendar's `source_shoot` column so the month stays traceable to real
work and you don't run dry.

## Evergreen vs shoot-driven

Not every post needs a fresh shoot. Educational, personal, and some social-proof
posts are **evergreen** — they can be written any time and slotted in when a week
is thin on new work. Keep a small backlog of evergreen ideas so a quiet shooting
month doesn't become a silent feed. Hero and behind-the-scenes are naturally
shoot-driven; the rest you can write from the desk.
