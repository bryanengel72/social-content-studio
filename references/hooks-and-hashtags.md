# Hooks and Hashtags

Two small things that decide whether a post is seen at all.

## The hook: the first line is the whole game

On every platform, the first line of the caption (or the first second of a Reel)
is what determines whether anyone reads the rest. A beautiful photo with a limp
first line — "Had the best time at this shoot!" — gets scrolled past. The hook has
one job: make stopping feel worth it.

Write three hook options for any post that matters and let the photographer pick.
Variety of angle beats polishing one line.

### Hook shapes that work

- **The specific stakes** — "A bad headshot costs you interviews you'll never know
  you lost." Names a real consequence.
- **The counter-intuitive** — "The best headshots aren't the ones where you're
  smiling." Creates a gap the reader needs closed.
- **The direct question that isn't lazy** — "When did you last update the photo on
  your company's About page?" Specific enough to sting a little.
- **The number/list promise** — "Three things every exec gets wrong before a
  headshot." Promises a payoff and a shape.
- **The behind-the-curtain** — "Here's what actually happens in the ten minutes
  before I press the shutter." Insider access.
- **The mid-story open** — "She almost cancelled that morning." Starts in motion,
  makes you want the rest.

### Hooks to avoid

"Happy Monday!", "Excited to share…", "Meet [name]!", "Loved this session" — these
are throat-clearing, not hooks. They tell the algorithm and the reader that nothing
is coming. Cut straight to the interesting thing.

The hook must also be **true to the post and the voice** — a clickbait hook the
content doesn't pay off costs trust, and a hook that isn't how the photographer
talks reads as borrowed. See [brand-voice.md](brand-voice.md).

## Hashtags: relevance and reach, not #love #instagood

Hashtags are how new people who aren't following yet find the post. The instinct is
to grab the biggest tags (#photography, 900M posts) where a new post drowns in
seconds. The better play is a **tiered mix**, weighted toward tags small enough to
actually rank in:

- **Broad** (1–2) — the category. #headshots, #brandphotography. Big, competitive,
  a long shot but occasionally hits.
- **Niche** (3–5) — specific to the work. #corporateheadshots, #executiveportrait,
  #personalbrandingphotos. Smaller, far likelier to surface you to the right person.
- **Local** (3–5) — the highest-value tags for most photographers, because clients
  book locally. #chicagoheadshots, #austinphotographer, #[neighborhood]. A small tag
  where you can genuinely rank and the searcher is a real prospect.
- **Branded** (1) — your own. #[businessname]. Builds a searchable body of work and
  catches people who already know you.

### Practical rules

- **Relevance over volume.** Ten precise tags beat thirty scattershot ones. Every
  tag should describe this post or this photographer, or it's noise that can hurt
  reach.
- **Rotate sets.** Reusing the identical block on every post is a known way to get
  throttled. Keep a few named sets (a corporate set, a local set, an educational
  set) and vary them. The calendar's `hashtag_set` column tracks which set a post
  used so you can rotate deliberately.
- **Local is the edge.** A wedding or headshot photographer competes globally on
  #wedding and locally on #denverweddingphotographer. The local tag is where the
  bookings actually come from — lead with those.
- **Never fabricate a location tag.** Only tag places the photographer actually
  serves. Tagging a city to poach its searches is the kind of thing that reads as
  spam and gets treated as such.
